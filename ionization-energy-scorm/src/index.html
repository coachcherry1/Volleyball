Successive ionization energies

 

The two-step rule

Step 1. Count the OCCUPIED energy levels. Different? That settles it. Fewer occupied levels: the electron is closer and held more tightly.
Step 2. Only if they are the same, compare the protons. More protons: a stronger pull. Inside one element the protons never change, so this is always a tie.

The next electron comes from…

the SAME occupied level
A small step up Both steps tie. The particle is more positive each time, so it costs a little more.
a LOWER occupied level
A huge jump The outer level is empty. The electron comes from the level underneath, closer to the nucleus.

The attraction is directly proportional to the nuclear charge.

The attraction is inversely proportional to the distance squared.

Reading the data

1. Find the huge jump One step far bigger than the rest. How many times bigger is it?
2. Count the ionization energies before it That is how many electrons were in the outer level.
3. Outer electrons → group 1 → group 1, 2 → group 2, 3 → group 13, 4 → group 14, 5 → group 15
4. Group and row → element
5. Check the configuration Its outermost occupied level should hold that many electrons.
