## Mass spectrum losses offered as labels

| Loss | Neutral | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M − 1 | •H | Loss of a single hydrogen atom. Small, but when M−1 is TALLER than M⁺ it means the fragment left behind is unusually stable — C₇H₇⁺ from toluene, the ring-plus-C=O fragment from benzaldehyde. |
| M − 15 | •CH₃ | Loss of a methyl radical. Look for a methyl sitting on a branch point or right next to a C=O — those are the two places a methyl leaves from. |
| M − 17 | •OH | Loss of a hydroxyl radical. In a carboxylic acid it is a big peak — the acid drops its •OH and keeps the C=O. In an alcohol it is usually much weaker than the −18 next to it. |
| M − 18 | H₂O | Loss of water — dehydration. This is an alcohol. M⁺ and M−18 together are the signature; in a tertiary alcohol the M⁺ may be gone entirely and only the −18 survives. |
| M − 27 | HCN | Loss of hydrogen cyanide, a whole small molecule. A nitrogen on or in a benzene ring — a nitrile or an aromatic amine — throws off HCN. The odd molecular ion said nitrogen; this says where it was. |
| M − 28 | CO or C₂H₄ | Loss of 28 is AMBIGUOUS: carbon monoxide, or a molecule of ethene. A ring that cannot lose a radical without breaking two bonds sheds a whole small molecule instead — CO from a phenol, C₂H₄ from a cycloalkane. The IR decides it: is there an O–H or a C=O? |
| M − 29 | •C₂H₅ or •CHO | Loss of 29 is AMBIGUOUS: an ethyl radical, or a formyl radical from an aldehyde. Decide it from the rest of the spectrum — an aldehyde also shows M−1, an ethyl group usually shows m/z 29 itself. |
| M − 31 | •OCH₃ or •CH₂OH | Loss of 31 is a methoxy radical from a methyl ester or ether — or a •CH₂OH from a primary alcohol. Both weigh 31, both are CH₃O. |
| M − 35 | •Cl | Loss of a chlorine atom. Check the M/M+2 pair first: if it was 3:1 there was a Cl to lose. |
| M − 36 | HCl | Loss of hydrogen chloride from an alkyl chloride — the chlorine takes a neighbouring hydrogen with it. |
| M − 43 | •C₃H₇ or CH₃CO• | Loss of 43 is AMBIGUOUS: a propyl radical, or an acetyl radical from a methyl ketone. Decide it from the rest of the spectrum — a methyl ketone normally shows a strong m/z 43 of its own. |
| M − 45 | •OC₂H₅ or •COOH | Loss of 45 is AMBIGUOUS: an ethoxy radical from an ethyl ester, or the whole •COOH group from a carboxylic acid. The IR settles it in one look — an acid has the enormous O–H envelope, an ester has none. |
| M − 46 | •NO₂ | Loss of a nitro group. The molecular ion was odd, so there is a nitrogen; losing 46 says it came as NO₂. The IR agrees: a very strong band near 1520. |
| M − 57 | •C₄H₉ or •COC₂H₅ | Loss of 57 runs the same trap as −29 and −43: a butyl radical, usually tert-butyl leaving a branch point, or an acyl radical from a ketone. Same question, bigger numbers. |
| M − 59 | •CO₂CH₃ | Loss of the whole methyl ester group, •COOCH₃. It leaves the bare ring behind when the ester sits on one — so a −59 next to a −31 from the same molecular ion is a methyl ester. |
| M − 79 | •Br | Loss of a bromine atom. Check the M/M+2 pair first: if it was 1:1 there was a Br to lose. |
